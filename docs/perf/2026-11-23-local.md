Issue #22. How the tests work: [README](README.md). Measured with `scripts/loadtest.sh` on the local stack, not on the
VM: nothing ran against staging or production.

- **Machine:** Windows 11 laptop, Docker Desktop 29.8.0 (amd64). **Images:** backend built from iltiam-backend `main`
  (`--build backend`), web `sha-aa7deb6` and AI `sha-a11a24d` (staging's tags), the fake segmentation model.
- **Data:** 500 patients and 60,000 image records (`scripts/loadtest.sh seed`), 50 doctors.
- **Runs:** 2026-11-21 (upload, capped runs) and 2026-11-23 (uncapped clinicians and analysis, 1.5-CPU run).
- **"Capped"** means the containers were limited with `docker update --cpus` to about the VM's 2 OCPUs in total. An
  amd64 CPU quota is not an Ampere core: the capped numbers show the direction, not the VM's exact figures.

## 3. Finding: on the VM, Postgres CPU decides the clinicians' NFR

Doubling the backend's CPU (A 0.75 → C 1.5) with Postgres kept at 0.5 CPU did not help (p95 5.9 → 5.6 s): the
dashboard, "needs attention", alerts and visit lists wait on the database with 60,000 visits. On the VM, staging and
production Postgres share the same 2 OCPUs.

What to do, in order:

1. Measure on the VM (README section 5, 5 users only) to see where the real figures fall between "uncapped" and A.
2. iltiam-backend (BE-23, iltiam-backend#89): the slowest pages are the dashboard, needs attention and visit detail;
   check their query plans against 60,000 visits (indexes on visit and assessment dates and status, the dashboard's
   60 s cache hit rate).
3. Split trigger for #19: if production's p95 for a page goes above 3 s while staging is busy, that is the "staging
   load hurts production" trigger.

## 4. Finding: 10 MB in 5 s on 4G needs a fast uplink or a smaller photo

| Uplink | Theoretical transfer of 9.9 MB | Measured p50 / p95 (server + link) |
|---|---|---|
| not shaped | 0 | 1.0 / 1.8 s |
| 40 Mbit/s | 2.0 s | 3.6 / 10.4 s |
| 20 Mbit/s | 4.0 s | 8.4 / 13.4 s |

The server is fast enough (p95 1.8 s for checking, cleaning and thumbnailing an 8.3 MP photo). The rest is physics:
80 Mbit at a typical 4G uplink of 10 to 20 Mbit/s takes 4 to 8 s before the server sees the last byte. Toxiproxy's
bandwidth limit is bursty, so the shaped p95s above are pessimistic, but even the theoretical figure fails at 20 Mbit/s.

The test photo is random pixels (the worst case); a real 8 MP phone JPEG is usually 2 to 4 MB, about 1 to 3 s at
10 to 20 Mbit/s. So the NFR holds for normal photos. To make it hold for any photo: the app compresses to at most
about 4 MB before upload (iltiam-mobile), or the NFR is restated in [SRS divergences](../srs-divergences.md) as
"< 5 s for the server, plus transfer". Decision for Thabit.

## 5. Finding: analysis end to end has a 3-second floor

With the fake model (milliseconds), half the analyses still took over 5 s and the p95 crossed 10 s on one of two runs.
The backend polls the AI service first after `AI_POLL_INTERVAL_S` (3 s) and then every 3 s (`apps/wounds/tasks.py`),
so even an instant result waits 3 s, and a result ready at 3.1 s waits until 6 s. The 9.9 MB upload itself adds
1 to 2 s. With the real model (p95 1.56 s on the VM, iltiam-ai-service#142) the first poll usually still misses.

Options for iltiam-backend: a first poll after 1 s and then backing off, or the AI service calling back. The 4 s
target needs one of them. Under the harshest cap (B) uploads queued behind each other on a quarter CPU and the end
to end reached minutes, which also shows that upload processing is CPU-heavy.
